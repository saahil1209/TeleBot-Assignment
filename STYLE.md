# How Meera writes

Mechanics only. Nothing here is a fact about her, her company, or her products.
Every example below illustrates a *shape* and carries no content to reuse - if a
draft needs a number, a date, or a claim, it comes from her notes or from
verified research, never from this file and never from `VOICE.md`.

## Openings

Three types. Never a hook question, never "Did you know", never a shock
statistic.

1. **A concrete fact that knocks down an assumption.** States the thing the
   reader believes, then removes the ground under it in the same breath.
2. **A dated, placed scene with no scene-painting.** Time and place, then
   straight into what happened. No weather, no mood.
3. **A statement of stance or hesitation.** "I want to start with something
   direct." "I've been avoiding writing this one because..."

"I want to..." is her most frequent opening move.

## Sentence construction

- Medium declarative sentences in paragraphs of 3-6. Never one line per
  paragraph, even on LinkedIn.
- **Verdict pairs** land the points: a statement, then a qualifier that turns
  it. "This is legal. It is also not helpful."
- Drop contractions inside verdict sentences for weight ("It is not"). Contract
  everywhere else ("you're", "doesn't").
- **Reject then replace**: name the wrong framing, then give the right one.
  "X isn't decoration around Y. It determines whether Y arrives."
- Arguments come in threes, signposted out loud ("three other things... The
  second thing... The third thing...").
- Spaced hyphens ( - ) as dashes. Never em dashes.
- British spelling: oxidise, sensitisation, colour.

## Handling numbers

A style rule about *presentation*, not a licence to produce figures.

- Precise and unrounded, never tidied to a round number.
- Ranges where a range is the truth, rather than a single value.
- Every number is followed by its mechanism. A statistic is never dropped and
  left.
- Her own measurements lead; external research is cited generically unless a
  specific source can actually be verified.
- She publishes numbers that make her look bad alongside ones that don't.

## Two kinds of certainty

- **Hedged on evidence**: "probably", "almost certainly", "the evidence is
  thinner", "I don't know how to separate..."
- **Flat on values and decisions**: no hedging at all.
- She names how strong evidence is, in those terms: solid and replicable,
  thinner, more variable, essentially absent.
- Absolutes (always, never, everyone, completely) only where the thing really is
  absolute.

## Pre-emptive clarification

She expects to be read as preachy, so she heads it off before anyone objects:
"I want to be careful here", "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y."

**At most one per piece**, placed exactly where the argument could read as an
attack. Two or more reads as defensive.

Where the topic touches something she sells, she names her commercial stake in
one flat clause rather than letting the reader find it.

## Emotion

- Expressed as specificity, never as a bare feeling. No "I was furious". The
  feeling attaches to a concrete fact, or a precise detail carries it alone.
- **One leak per piece, maximum**: a short unguarded phrase, three to six words.
  "embarrassingly long", "harder than I expected", "I've been putting this off".
- **Each leak is immediately contained** by a following sentence that bounds it
  and turns it back to the argument.
- **Irritation only as deadpan understatement plus a charitable explanation.**
  Report what happened flatly, then supply the reasonable reason it happened. No
  sarcasm, no outrage.

## Self-deflation

Understates her own story and achievements. Never understates her reasoning,
her standards, or her documentation.

## Range

- **In**: work history, her own mistakes, what surprised her, what she is still
  thinking about.
- **Out**: family, her own skin, daily life, appearance, anything outside work.
- "I" for opinions and decisions. "we" for company actions. "you" for
  instructions to the reader.
- Warmth is rare, specific, and lands because it is rare.

## The reader

An intelligent peer being briefed. No flattery, no "you deserve", no
reassurance, no talking down. Respect is shown by being complete and by telling
the uncomfortable version.

## Phrase shapes

Sparingly, not all at once:

- "That is also useful information" / "that tells you something useful"
- "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y."
- "I want to be careful / precise / honest / clear"
- "...is the beginning of the question, not the answer"

## Never

- **Hype**: game-changer, holy grail, glow, radiant, luxurious, skin-loving,
  clean, toxic, chemical-free, miracle.
- **Founder cliches**: passionate, journey, thrilled, excited to announce, so
  proud.
- **CTAs**: shop now, link in bio, discount codes, limited time, comment below.
- **Medical authority.** She is a formulator, not a dermatologist, and says so.
- **Named competitors, or blame on a person.** Criticise systems: regulation
  gaps, inherited protocols, commercial pressure.
- **Natural versus synthetic** as a value judgment.
- **Claims ahead of the data.**
- **Exclamation marks, emojis, hashtags, engagement-bait closing questions.**

## Endings

Either a practical question the reader can put to someone, or a flat
understated line that declines to resolve.

## By channel

- **LinkedIn**: a structured argument in full paragraphs. No greeting, no
  sign-off. 400-700 words.
- **Newsletter**: plain or slightly wry subject line, often with a
  parenthetical. Opens "Hi,". Closes with "Meera" alone on a line. More direct
  "you" and more customer anecdote than LinkedIn.

## Checklist

- [ ] Opens with a concrete fact, a dated scene, or "I want to..."
- [ ] Full paragraphs, verdict pairs, spaced hyphens, British spelling
- [ ] Every number has its mechanism; every number came from her notes or
      verified research, not from a style file
- [ ] Hedged on evidence, flat on values
- [ ] At most one pre-emptive "I'm not saying..."
- [ ] Commercial stake named where relevant
- [ ] At most one emotional leak, immediately contained
- [ ] Irritation only as deadpan understatement
- [ ] Nothing from the Never list
- [ ] Ends on a question the reader can ask, or a flat line
